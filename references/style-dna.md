# Style DNA

Translate the user's style choice into observable rendering decisions. A label such as “Vector,” “3D,” or “editorial” is only the family name; it is not sufficient art direction.

## Build the DNA

Resolve these fields after the user chooses a style or supplies a style reference:

| Field | Decide |
| --- | --- |
| Medium | The physical or digital process the image should resemble |
| Form language | Geometric, organic, angular, soft, exaggerated, restrained |
| Mark or surface | Flat fill, ink line, grain, brushwork, paper fiber, clay, metal, skin, glass |
| Edge behavior | Crisp, cut, dry-brush, feathered, imperfect, depth-softened |
| Depth model | Flat layering, shallow relief, sculptural depth, photographic space |
| Light model | Graphic shadow, diffuse daylight, studio key, chiaroscuro, ambient glow with a source |
| Palette behavior | Dominant family, secondary family, scarce accent, contrast level |
| Detail rhythm | Large quiet masses versus localized fine detail |
| Type relationship | Integrated, spatially aligned, embedded, or intentionally absent |
| Avoid | Two to five style-specific failure modes likely for this brief |

Lock recurring traits, not sample content. Never copy the reference image's subject, exact composition, signature artwork, watermark, or brand.

## Medium calibrations

### Vector

Use authored silhouettes, intentional geometry, controlled curves, limited value steps, and graphic shadows. Permit restrained grain only when it supports the chosen identity. Avoid flat-icon packs, corporate explainer characters, uniform outlines, and unrelated decorative shapes.

### 3D

Specify material logic, sculptural proportions, lens or spatial depth, and motivated studio light. Keep one material family dominant. Avoid glossy toy defaults, plastic faces, game-render scenery, and bloom without a source.

### Editorial or hand-drawn illustration

Specify mark-making: ink, gouache, pencil, screen print, dry brush, or painterly digital strokes. Keep shape exaggeration purposeful and the composition designed. Avoid synthetic clip-art, uniform digital smoothness, and random texture overlays.

### Collage

Specify the source language: cut paper, archival photograph, torn fiber, photocopy, print registration, or tactile assemblage. Every fragment must participate in the metaphor. Avoid asset piles, scrapbook filler, and unrelated ephemera.

### Photography

Direct one physically credible scene: casting, gesture, set, prop relationship, lens distance, light source, material response, and controlled color. Avoid generic stock behavior, anonymous smiling groups, and impossible composites disguised as photographs.

### Typography-led

Make the word-form perform the conceptual verb through scale, interruption, compression, reflection, erosion, folding, or spatial interaction. Avoid text pasted over decoration or effects that do not change meaning.

## Reference-image extraction

When a reference defines the style, extract only:

1. medium and surface;
2. form and edge language;
3. depth and lighting behavior;
4. palette ratios and contrast;
5. density, negative space, and typographic relationship.

Convert those observations into a reusable recipe for the new concept. Do not name a living artist as the output target; describe observable traits instead.
